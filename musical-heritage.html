Musical Heritage

The Indore Gharana

In Hindustani classical music, a gharana represents more than a particular style of singing. It is a living artistic tradition passed from teacher to student, carrying distinctive approaches to raag, melody, rhythm, repertoire, improvisation, and musical expression.

My musical heritage is deeply rooted in the Indore Gharana, pioneered and shaped by the legendary Ustad Amir Khan (1912–1974). One of the most influential vocalists of twentieth-century Hindustani classical music, Amir Khan created an extraordinary musical language by drawing upon different traditions while developing an aesthetic that was unmistakably his own. His approach brought together the spaciousness and gravity of Dhrupad, the melodic depth of Khayal, the intricate possibilities of merukhand, and an exceptional sensitivity to poetry and text.

Amir Khan's music was revolutionary in its restraint. He moved away from virtuosity as display and instead allowed the raag to unfold slowly, patiently, and with profound concentration. His use of ati-vilambit laya, expansive melodic development, long meends, carefully placed pauses, and a deep emphasis on the lower and middle registers created an atmosphere that could be both meditative and intensely emotional. His belief in preserving the purity and integrity of the raag was central to this aesthetic.

"A note does not need to be hurried to be powerful, and silence can be as expressive as sound."

Discovering Amir Khan's music changed my understanding of what it means to sing — that the emotional world of a raag can unfold gradually, and that technical mastery is not the destination, but a means of reaching something deeper and more personal through music.

A path of training

1982

Formal training begins

My connection to this tradition began with Vidushi Amarjeet Kaur, a senior disciple of Ustad Amir Khan.

1982–1995

Within the tradition

For more than a decade, I had the privilege of learning within this musical-heritage — absorbing not only its musical vocabulary, but its discipline, aesthetics, approach to poetry, and way of listening.

Ongoing

Study with Pandit Vinayak Torvi

Subsequent study with Pandit Vinayak Torvi further expanded my understanding of Hindustani music, while my foundation in the Indore tradition remained central to my artistic identity.

Over more than four decades, Amir Khan's music has continued to influence my musical journey — not as a model to imitate, but as a guiding artistic philosophy. It has shaped the way I approach raag, phrasing, rhythm, poetry, and ultimately, the relationship between the musician and the listener.

A Living Heritage

Rooted, and always evolving

I believe that musical heritage is not something static to be preserved unchanged. A tradition remains alive when it is understood deeply, experienced authentically, and allowed to find meaning in the present.

In my singing, I continue to explore the Indore Gharana's emphasis on expansive melodic development, the emotional depth of raag, expressive meend, and the intimate relationship between poetry and melody. I am particularly drawn to the way Amir Khan brought profound poetry into the musical experience, allowing the words of a composition to become an integral part of its emotional expression.

This philosophy also informs my own compositions, written under the pen name Ibaadat. Rather than simply recreating the musical language I inherited, I seek to carry its underlying principles into new compositions and contemporary contexts — allowing the tradition to evolve while remaining rooted in its aesthetic foundation.

My engagement with musical heritage has also extended beyond the traditional concert stage. I have collaborated with museums, universities, and cultural institutions across North America, developing performances, workshops, and interdisciplinary programs that bring Hindustani music into dialogue with visual art, history, spirituality, and other cultural traditions.

My work has also included collaborations and explorations involving European Medieval and early music, creating opportunities to discover connections between musical traditions that developed in very different historical and cultural environments.

These experiences have reinforced my belief that musical traditions, however different their origins, can meet through shared human experiences — poetry, devotion, contemplation, storytelling, beauty, and the search for meaning.

The Indore Gharana is the foundation of my musical identity. Ustad Amir Khan's music changed the way I listen, the way I sing, and the way I understand the purpose of music.

My responsibility now is to honour what has been entrusted to me while continuing to explore how this profound tradition can speak to listeners today and to generations yet to come. A tradition lives not only when it is preserved, but when it continues to speak to the present.
